04 / 07 — Editorial · Bookbinding · Writing

Body
of Work

‘How do tattoos aid identity formation and group solidarity?’ - written by hand, bound by hand, and made physical.

Research / writingEditorial objectJapanese stab binding
01 - The problem

A body argument, flattened into a document.

A standard printed dissertation strips the physicality out of its subject.

02 - My role

Researcher, writer and maker.

I wrote 25,000 words, photographed and interviewed a range of people by myself, designed the editorial language and made the publication by hand.

03 - The outcome

Where form becomes the thesis.

An editorial object, not a printout: the making is part of the argument.

A body argument, flattened into a document. Then made physical again.

Object as argument

I hand-wrote the dissertation in ink to replicate the ink used in tattooing. I used Japanese binding needles to echo the tattoo needle, so the making process physically repeats the subject of the research. The photographs and interviews were also carried out by me, keeping the body of the author and the people studied present in the final object.

Publication objectLayered surfaces replicate skin and the layers that make us human - set against the mark-making that first tested the form.
Group and selfIdentity repeated, displayed and read through the body - the personal image assembled through fragments.
